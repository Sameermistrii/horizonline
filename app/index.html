How it works

Sun and Earth positions come from the NASA/JPL Horizons System, hourly for 2027 to 2029, already corrected for the Moon's libration. The horizon around each site is traced through NASA LRO laser-altimeter (LOLA) terrain, including the curve of the Moon; there is no atmosphere to bend light. Power counts the share of the solar disk above the terrain; Earth link counts hours when Earth's centre is above it.

Checks: Sun and Earth positions derived for any site match Horizons' own per-site output within 0.0002°. Every Artemis III region shows at least 17 days of continuous sunlight in 2027, consistent with NASA's 6.5-day selection requirement.

Limits: curated sites use 80 m terrain; custom sites use 240 m terrain and can read up to about 10 points sunnier on sharp peaks. Terrain beyond 80°S is not modelled, so Chandrayaan-3 and Apollo 11 use a smooth Moon. Landing safety (slope, rocks) is not assessed.

Data: NASA LRO Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter (LOLA) south-pole terrain, NASA GSFC Planetary Geodynamics Data Archive; Sun and Earth positions from the NASA/JPL Horizons System; Artemis III region boxes from USGS (doi:10.5066/P1MEQ6UK).
